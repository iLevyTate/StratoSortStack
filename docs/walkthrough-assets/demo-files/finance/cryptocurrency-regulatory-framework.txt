Global Cryptocurrency Regulatory Framework — Comparative Analysis 2026

Prepared by: Brookings Institution, Digital Assets Policy Program
Authors: Dr. Timothy Massad, Esme Hawkins, J.D.
Published: August 2026

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The global regulatory landscape for digital assets tightened another notch in
the twelve months after the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regime
settled. This paper compares cryptocurrency frameworks across 12 major
jurisdictions, covering consumer protection, anti-money laundering, stablecoin
oversight, and DeFi governance as of mid-2026.

STABLECOIN REGULATION DEEP DIVE

Global stablecoin market cap: $214 billion (Aug 2026)
  - USDT (Tether): $108B (50%)
  - USDC (Circle): $68B (32%)
  - DAI (MakerDAO / Sky): $9.1B (4%)
  - Others: $28.9B (14%)

MiCA Requirements (EU):
  - 1:1 reserve backing in high-quality liquid assets (cash, government bonds)
  - Reserves held at EU credit institutions (30% minimum in cash)
  - Quarterly reserve attestations by independent auditors
  - Daily transaction cap for non-euro significant asset-referenced tokens
  - Tether (USDT): still non-compliant for EU CASP listing as of June 2026

US Approach:
  - OCC interpretive letter (Jan 2025): National banks may issue stablecoins
  - Payment-stablecoin bills continue to require 100% reserves and monthly
    attestations, with a large-issuer threshold for Fed oversight
  - Status: Committee work complete; floor calendar still fluid as of Q3 2026

DECENTRALIZED FINANCE (DeFi) CHALLENGES

Total Value Locked (DeFi): $172 billion (Aug 2026)
Top protocols: Lido ($41B), Aave ($26B), Sky/Maker ($19B), Uniswap ($13B)

Regulatory questions unresolved:
1. Who is the "issuer" or "operator" of a decentralized protocol?
2. Can smart contract code constitute a "prospectus" or "white paper"?
3. How do KYC/AML requirements apply to non-custodial protocols?
4. Jurisdiction over DAOs with globally distributed token holders?

EU approach: Commission follow-up (published H1 2026) still points toward
"same activity, same risk, same regulation" with guidance for identifiable
protocol operators rather than a MiCA rewrite.

  - Pig-butchering scams: $4.8B (30%)
  - Rug pulls / exit scams: $3.2B (19%)
  - Phishing / social engineering: $3.0B (19%)
  - Ponzi / yield schemes: $2.6B (16%)

POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Establish international stablecoin standards through FSB/IOSCO coordination.
2. Adopt activity-based (not entity-based) regulation for DeFi protocols.
3. Create regulatory sandboxes for tokenized real-world assets (RWA).
4. Mandate on-chain analytics for AML compliance.
5. Harmonize tax reporting (OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework by 2027).
