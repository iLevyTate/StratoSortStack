Amazon Deforestation and Its Economic Impact on Global Carbon Markets

World Resources Institute | Policy Brief No. 44, July 2026

SUMMARY
Between 2001 and 2025, the Brazilian Amazon lost approximately 236,000 km² of
primary forest—an area larger than the United Kingdom. This policy brief
quantifies the economic externalities of continued deforestation through the
lens of carbon credit markets, agricultural commodity supply chains, and
ecosystem service valuation.

CARBON ECONOMICS
  Carbon stored in Amazon biomass: ~150 billion tonnes CO₂e
  Annual emissions from deforestation (2021–2025 avg): 1.05 Gt CO₂e
  Social Cost of Carbon (US EPA, 2025): $230/tonne CO₂e
  Implied annual externality: $241 billion

The voluntary carbon market valued Amazonian REDD+ credits at $9–16/tonne in
2025, a fraction of the social cost. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism
(CBAM) is now in force (2026) and raises the effective price of embedded
deforestation carbon in agricultural exports to the EU toward ~€90/tonne.

AGRICULTURAL TRADE IMPACTS
Brazil exported $49B in soybeans and $13B in beef in 2025. The EU Deforestation
Regulation (EUDR) now requires supply-chain traceability to plot-level
coordinates. Compliance costs are estimated at $1.3B for the Brazilian
agricultural sector; non-compliance still risks more than $6B in annual EU
market access.

RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Align carbon credit pricing with social cost of carbon through compliance
   market integration.
2. Support Brazil's PPCDAm enforcement with $500M multilateral funding.
3. Finish EUDR technical assistance for smallholders in the 2026–27 window.
4. Invest in high-resolution satellite monitoring (Planet, NICFI partnership).
